Your Place · Data

    Your city, through data

    Open data about a city on a 3D model: how steep its streets are, how busy, how its trams and buses run, what homes cost and what people earn. Search a place, then pick a layer.

    Steepness

    Roads and paths coloured by how steep they are, anywhere. Search a place above to see it.

    Traffic

    Cars and bikes counted every hour at hundreds of points, as red streets that rise with the traffic. Only in Hamburg so far.

    Public transport

    Trams, buses, trains and ferries moving through the square as your city’s timetable has them. Search your city, then find its timetable in the panel, or load a timetable file (GTFS); it stays on your device.

    Homes

    What homes cost, as building colours: rent per m² or home values, per neighbourhood or 100 m square. Find open data for your place in the panel, or load a file; it stays on your device.

    Income

    What people earn, as building colours, per neighbourhood or district where statistics offices publish it. Find open data for your place in the panel, or load a file.

    Your route

    Load a run, a ride or a race (GPX or TCX) and see it over any layer, with its height profile and steepest climbs. The file stays on your device.

    More layers are on the way: water, energy.

      Streets near a counting point (the white dots) rise and turn red with the traffic in the hour you pick. Each count spreads along its street and fades out over the distance you set; where counts meet, as at a crossing, they add up. Only the dots are counted: in between it’s an estimate. Tap a dot to see its day. The last 24 hours, Hamburg time.

      Traffic counts: Freie und Hansestadt Hamburg, Behörde für Verkehr und Mobilitätswende (dl-de/by-2-0), live from iot.hamburg.de.

        From the Mobility Database, an open list of public transport timetables worldwide.

        Not listed? Transit operators publish their timetables as GTFS files (ZIP), often on their website or an open data portal. The file stays on this device. It’s never uploaded.

            Vehicles move as the timetable plans them, not live: delays and cancellations don’t show. Tap a vehicle or a line to see that line on its own. U-Bahn and metro lines are dashed and their trains see-through, as most run underground.

            This file has no line shapes, so lines run straight from stop to stop.
